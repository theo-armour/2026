# 2026-06-19 Who Am I

I read a lot of stories and articles on Medium and Substack, as well as a lot of other journals. Once I've found a writer that I like, after reading a few of their articles, I tend to go to their About page to see who they say they are.

I find there are a lot of nearly empty about pages, way too many for my taste. I find this is quite sadActually, I think it's a much more meaningful aspect. Because I think perhaps it means the author hasn't really ever responded to the question that they might ask themselves, "Who am I?"

So if nothing else, a nice about page is a way of sharing with other people that deep question that one really ought to ask oneself from time to time. Who am I?

There's a wonderful example of people who do this regularly.And that includes some of the smartest people in the world. When a good computer program writes a program, they usually include, right at the top, a README file. The README file is a way of saying, "This is what I am. This is what I do. This is how I work. This is how you can use me." It's a way of sharing with the world who they are and what they do. It's letting the program they have written speak for itself.

I program computer software, and so I know a lot about README files.

The issue with writing a good About Me page is that it's very easy to procrastinate starting the effort because, after all, there is still a long way to go before it (your life) finishes.

The other issue is that the moment you start writing about something concrete and specific and something you like and you're good at and have experience at, yet another thing, like a similar thing, comes up and you haven't begun to think about that one.And so off you go into a rabbit hole of thoughts from all over the place, and nothing ever gets done.

So what we need is a simple way of getting going. We want to stand on the shoulders of giants and find out how other people have started projects like this.

So, below are some different ideas that I've come up with to help you ask that eternal wonderful question. Who am I?

Wait, one more thing

I completely forgot. Strongest writing is when you write for yourself. So, from time to time, why not be a narcissist and have a bit of ego? Let yourself go. Write about yourself for yourself.

Perhaps the most wonderful thing about writing your own about page is that It is a wonderful way of doing something nice for yourself by getting a good start on that eternal question. Who am I?

## A Usable and Enlightening About Me page

Now that we've established that you really neeed A pretty darn good about page. What might be some tips and tricks for getting going?

## Five Ws
* Five Ws: Who, What, When, Where, Why. This is a good way to start your About page. It will help your readers understand who you are and why they should read your articles.
* https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Ws
* Media Diet: Who do you like to read? Five Ws?
* Current Project and Interests: What are you currently interested in? This will help your readers understand what to expect from your articles.
