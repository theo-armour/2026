# 2026-06-13 Finance Writing and the depth of engagement:

"Literature is the art of writing something that will be read twice; journalism what will be grasped at once."
— Cyril Connolly (Karhio, 2014)

My friend writes finance articles on Substack. Let's call him Ell. The Ell writes articles about stocks finds interesting stocks to write about. He writes extremely well and has lots of interesting things to say about the stocks.

Nonetheless, something in my brain doesn't really click. A lot of it has to do with financial writing in general. Anything to do with a stock and the company it represents is a almost always a very complicated, very timely matter. So whatever you write about a stock today may be out of date or simply wrong tomorrow. There is nothing particularly wrong about this sort of writing. Many people like to read articles like this. It provides morning entertainment for millions of people.

So my question to myself is: suppose I am tasked with maintaining a blog and having to come up with regular blog posts on finance. How would I make this amusing and challenging for myself?

>
> Not relevant or perhaps an aside.
>
>I suppose I'm a bit more greedy in my reading, and I want to read something because it's a good reason for doing it today, tomorrow, and the next day. There is an idea or a concept that I have in my brain, and this article serves to justify it or even disagree with it.
>
>Articles that I read should help me adjust my bias, come up with new feelings, or somehow make a difference in the larger patterns of my life. They should be helping me build up greater mental models and ideas that will help me live an even better life than I am now. ha ha. I wish this were true, but you get where I'm heading toward.
>
>So if I were writing financial articles, I think I would like to have some kind of overall strategy for investing. Every article would be an instance of me analyzing something to see how it matched up with some rules and ideas and tools that I had previously created.
>
>So I might have something like: I have an idea about why a stock is a good stock, and then I would run some facts and figures about the current stock, which would serve to justify my statement.
>
>I would have a strategy for investing. My posts would be instances of trying to put that strategy into play and seeing if the strategy was working and if the symbol was actually part of that fit into that strategy. And perhaps even more fun might be the article on showing where my thesis was wrong and how I was wrong about it.
>
>So, thinking out loud, I would like to have something like 30 different analytical parameters and use AI and the numbers available online to see how well the issue I was writing about met those parameters.
>
>But of course this kind of thinking is somewhat out of date because now we have AI. AI could help come up with the parameters and be able to monitor those parameters on a daily, if not hourly, basis. But writing about AI and investment is a whole can of worm, so let's put that aside for simply enjoying reading other articles for the fun of it.

### EAT (Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness) Compliance

Apart from an investment strategy, there is also the simple matter of good writing. I would like to see financial writing that is more engaging, very well referenced, and authoritative whenever possible.

* Stand on the shoulders of giants. Make article EAT (Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness) compliant by referencing original sources and providing links to them.

### Identify the players and their position

* Identify the sector and of course, important sub sector. Example: tech/component assembly
* Identify why that sector is interesting at this moment.
* Identify several of the major players in that sector

### Identify aspects that will help your readers know that this is written by a human for humans

* The symbol of the stock should be added to the name of any company with a symbol that's of interest.
* If the company has a Wikipedia entry, Link to its article  should also be included.
* If there are technical terms of interest, a link to their Wikipedia entry should be included.
